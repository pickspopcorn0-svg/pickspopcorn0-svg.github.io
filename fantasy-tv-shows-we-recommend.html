7 Picks Magic Mythology World-Building Epic

Fantasy television offers escape into worlds where magic is real, ancient prophecies matter, and the impossible becomes everyday. These shows transport you to realms of wonder, danger, and discovery.

Below is a curated selection of fantasy series that deliver compelling worlds, rich mythology, and characters worth following through darkness and light. Each brings something different to the genre, but all share that essential quality: they make you believe.

01 of 07

The Wheel of Time

2021–Present

The Wheel of Time arrives with the weight of epic fantasy expectations and largely delivers on the promise of Robert Jordan's massive book series. This is high fantasy at full scale: a world where magic exists but comes at terrible cost, where ancient evils stir after centuries of sleep, and where a small group of villagers discover they're caught in prophecies that will reshape everything.

The story follows Moiraine, a member of the powerful all-female Aes Sedai order, as she arrives in a remote village searching for the Dragon Reborn, a prophesied figure who will either save the world or break it. Five young people from the village become her focus, and when dark forces attack, they're forced to flee into a world far larger and more dangerous than they ever imagined.

What makes The Wheel of Time compelling is its willingness to explore the complexity of magic. The One Power can be wielded by women through the Aes Sedai, but men who touch it go mad, corrupted by an ancient taint. This creates a world where power is gendered, where institutional structures have formed around controlling magic, and where the very thing that might save humanity is also deeply dangerous. The show doesn't shy away from these contradictions. It builds its drama around them.

The production values are ambitious. From the sprawling city of Tar Valon to the haunted ruins of ancient civilizations, the show creates a world that feels lived-in and layered. It's not perfect; some storylines feel rushed. But when it works, it captures that essential fantasy feeling: the sense that you're watching a world with depths you haven't yet explored, where every revelation hints at larger mysteries still waiting.

The Originals

2013–2018 · 5 Seasons

The Originals takes the vampire mythology established in The Vampire Diaries and transforms it into something darker, more operatic, and deeply rooted in family drama. This is a show about the first vampires, the Mikaelson siblings, and their thousand-year history of love, betrayal, and survival. It's Gothic fantasy with a Southern Gothic setting, and it fully commits to both.

Klaus Mikaelson returns to New Orleans, the city his family helped build centuries ago, only to find it's been taken over by his former protégé. What starts as a power play becomes something more complicated when Klaus discovers he's going to be a father — an impossibility for a vampire — and a development that changes everything.

The show's greatest strength is its focus on family as both salvation and curse. The Mikaelsons are immortal, unkillable, and absolutely toxic to each other. Klaus is a monster who desperately wants to be loved. Elijah is noble to a fault, constantly sacrificing his own happiness to protect his brother. Rebekah just wants to be free of both of them. Their dynamic is messy, painful, and utterly compelling.

New Orleans itself becomes a character. The show uses the city's real history — its French Quarter architecture, its voodoo traditions, its complicated cultural legacy — to build a supernatural underworld that feels authentic. The Originals doesn't reinvent the vampire genre, but it refines it, maintaining that central question: can people who've done terrible things for a thousand years ever truly change?

A Discovery of Witches

2018–2022 · 3 Seasons

A Discovery of Witches is fantasy for people who like their magic steeped in history, academia, and slow-burning romance. Based on Deborah Harkness's All Souls trilogy, it follows Diana Bishop, a historian and reluctant witch who discovers an enchanted manuscript in Oxford's Bodleian Library — a book that's been lost for centuries and that every magical creature in the world seems desperate to find.

Diana has spent her adult life suppressing her magic, trying to live as a normal scholar. But when the manuscript chooses her, she's thrown into a hidden world of witches, vampires, and daemons, all governed by an ancient covenant that forbids interspecies relationships. Enter Matthew Clairmont, a centuries-old vampire and geneticist drawn to Diana in a way that violates every rule their world has established.

What makes the show work is how seriously it takes its intellectual side. This isn't just magic — it's magic as science, history, and philosophy. Matthew approaches Diana's powers as a research problem. Diana uncovers magical secrets hidden in medieval alchemical texts. The show spends real time in libraries, laboratories, and historical settings, and trusts that viewers will find that compelling. It's fantasy for people who appreciate research montages as much as spell-casting.

The romance is deliberate and earned. Their relationship develops slowly, built on mutual respect and genuine curiosity about each other. When they time-travel to Elizabethan London in the second season, the show fully leans into historical fantasy. A Discovery of Witches is measured and patient — not for everyone, but if you want fantasy that feels literate and treats magic as something ancient and complex, this delivers exactly that.

The Shannara Chronicles

2016–2017 · 2 Seasons

The Shannara Chronicles takes Terry Brooks's post-apocalyptic fantasy novels and turns them into a brisk, visually striking adventure series. Set thousands of years after our world ended, it follows a reality where magic has returned, elves rule the Four Lands, and an ancient tree called the Ellcrys is the only thing keeping demonic forces imprisoned. When the tree begins to die, a young elf princess, a half-elf wanderer, and a human rover must find a way to save it.

The show's most distinctive element is its setting. This isn't generic medieval fantasy. It's a world built on the ruins of ours. Characters travel through overgrown cities where skyscrapers have become forests. Ancient human technology is treated as mysterious relics. The blending of post-apocalyptic imagery with high fantasy gives the show a unique visual identity that sets it apart from everything else in the genre.

The core trio carries the story. Amberle is the first female chosen by the Ellcrys in generations. Wil is a half-elf carrying magical stones as dangerous as they are powerful. Eretria is a human rover raised among thieves, pulled into the quest against her better judgment. Their dynamic is classic adventure storytelling: three people from different worlds forced to trust each other against impossible odds.

The Shannara Chronicles is unabashedly young adult fantasy and commits fully to that energy. The pacing is quick, the tone is earnest, and it knows what it is. Where it stumbles is in depth — the world-building hints at fascinating ideas but rarely fully explores them. But for accessible, fast-paced fantasy with striking visuals and a clear hero's journey, it delivers without pretension.

The Order

2019–2020 · 2 Seasons

The Order is fantasy with a sharp sense of humor about itself. Set at the fictional Belgrave University, it follows Jack Morton, a student who joins a secret magical society called the Hermetic Order of the Blue Rose, only to discover that magic is real, powerful, and deeply irresponsible in the hands of entitled undergraduates. Things get more complicated when he's also chosen by a werewolf hide, making him part of the Knights of Saint Christopher — now a double agent in a war he barely understands.

The show's tone is its defining feature. It's not self-serious or brooding. It's sarcastic, fast-paced, and fully aware of how ridiculous its premise is. The Order is basically a magical fraternity where students learn spells, perform rituals, and accidentally unleash demonic forces while dealing with crushes and rivalries. The werewolves are a dysfunctional found family who bicker constantly. The comedy doesn't undercut the stakes; it just makes the whole thing more fun to watch.

Jack is a solid protagonist because he's genuinely in over his head. He joined the Order for revenge, his mother was a member who died under mysterious circumstances, but he quickly realizes that magic is far more dangerous and morally complicated than he anticipated. His integration into the werewolf crew provides most of the show's best character dynamics — people who shouldn't work together but somehow do.

The Order only ran for two seasons before being canceled, ending on a cliffhanger. That's frustrating, but the show still works as a contained experience. If you want fantasy that doesn't take itself too seriously, mixes horror, comedy, and campus intrigue, and treats its supernatural premise as a playground rather than a burden, this is a fun, bingeable watch.

06 of 07

Shadowhunters

2016–2019 · 3 Seasons

Shadowhunters is urban fantasy built on a foundation of mythology, chosen-one narratives, and deeply complicated family drama. Based on Cassandra Clare's Mortal Instruments series, it follows Clary Fray, a Brooklyn art student who discovers she's part of a secret world of half-angel warriors who protect humanity from demons and other supernatural threats. Her mother's disappearance forces Clary into this hidden world, where she learns her family history is far darker than she ever imagined.

The show throws you immediately into a richly developed supernatural ecosystem. Shadowhunters operate wielding angelic runes that grant them enhanced abilities. Downworlders — vampires, werewolves, warlocks, and faeries — live in uneasy coexistence governed by treaties and ancient prejudices. The politics are messy, the alliances are fragile, and everyone has conflicting loyalties. It's a world with real depth.

Clary's journey from ordinary teenager to powerful Shadowhunter is familiar territory, but the show commits to it fully. Alec and Magnus — a Shadowhunter struggling with his identity and a centuries-old warlock with impeccable style — develop one of the show's most compelling relationships, treated with real care and groundbreaking for representation in the genre.

Shadowhunters is melodramatic in the way young adult fantasy often is. Some of it works; some feels overwrought. But the show has genuine heart, and it's unapologetically romantic. If you want urban fantasy that fully embraces its heightened emotions and complex mythology, Shadowhunters delivers without hesitation.

07 of 07

Merlin

2008–2012 · 5 Seasons

Merlin reimagines the Arthurian legend as a coming-of-age story about a young warlock and a young prince who are destined to change the world if they don't drive each other crazy first. Set in Camelot, where magic is banned on pain of death, Merlin must protect Arthur while hiding his powers completely — because revealing them would mean execution.

The genius of the show is making Merlin and Arthur contemporaries rather than mentor and student. They start as antagonists — Merlin thinks Arthur is a spoiled bully, Arthur thinks Merlin is an incompetent servant. But over five seasons, they become brothers in all but blood. Their friendship is the emotional core, built on trust, loyalty, and the tragic irony that Arthur can never know the truth about Merlin, even as Merlin uses magic constantly to save his life.

The show takes a deliberately accessible approach. Episodes largely follow a monster-of-the-week structure where Merlin and Arthur face magical threats ranging from cursed knights to mythical beasts. It's family-friendly without being juvenile, balancing humor and heart with genuine danger. The tone is lighter than most modern fantasy, but the stakes are real.

What gives Merlin staying power is its willingness to embrace tragedy. This is a story where you know how it ends. Camelot falls, Arthur dies, magic returns too late. The show plays with that knowledge the entire time, and the final season commits fully to the darker aspects of the legend. The ending is heartbreaking in exactly the way great myths should be. Comfort-food fantasy with genuine emotional weight.
