Best Chinese Fantasy Dramas

If you are new to C drama fantasy, here is the easiest way to think about it. Xianxia is gods, immortals, reincarnation, realms, and big destiny love stories. Wuxia fantasy is the martial world, sect rivalries, reputation, and people trying to stay human while carrying a sword. Cultivation is growth, training, power levels, and earning your place the hard way.

These picks are here because they each have a clear vibe. You will know what you are signing up for before you start.

Listening Snow Tower

2019 • 56 episodes
Wuxia fantasyMartial worldRomanceTragedy vibes

This is wuxia that takes itself seriously. The martial world in Listening Snow Tower is not a backdrop for fun adventures. It is a place where sects have long memories, alliances shift without warning, and trust is always conditional. The tone is cold from the beginning and it stays that way, which is exactly what makes it worth recommending if that mood is what you are after.

The fight sequences are there for a reason. Each confrontation feels like something building or something breaking rather than spectacle for its own sake. The story keeps reminding you that strength in this world is never enough on its own. Reputation, loyalty, and timing matter just as much, and the characters who forget that pay for it.

The romance is present but restrained. It runs underneath the plot like a slow current rather than sitting on top of everything. That actually suits the tone better than a louder love story would. Two people who care for each other in a world that does not allow much softness creates more tension than any grand declaration could.

Watch it if you want a martial world story with a brooding atmosphere, genuine emotional weight, and a romance that earns its moments instead of demanding them.

Main cast: Qin Junjie, Yuan Bingyan
Best for: people who want a colder, moodier martial world story.
Not for: viewers who need bright energy and guaranteed happy endings.

Ancient Love Poetry

2021 • 49 episodes
XianxiaGodsLong time loveFate and sacrifice

Ancient Love Poetry commits fully to the xianxia formula and delivers it well. Gods, multiple realms, love that spans impossible timelines, and a relationship that gets tested in every direction before it is allowed to breathe. The early episodes build the world properly, which means some patience is needed at the start, but once the story finds its emotional engine the momentum does not let up.

What separates this from lighter xianxia romance is that the love here carries weight. Choosing each other has consequences. The sacrifices are real and change how the characters relate to each other afterward. That gives even the slower stretches a sense of pressure underneath, because you know every moment of peace is temporary.

The drama also handles the long timeline well. Watching a relationship shift from early warmth to forced distance to earned reunion is the whole point of this format, and Ancient Love Poetry understands that the middle section, where things go wrong, is what makes the resolution matter. It does not rush through the difficult parts just to get to the payoff.

Pick this when you want xianxia romance that goes big, takes its emotional beats seriously, and makes you feel the cost of love rather than just the sweetness of it.

Main cast: Zhou Dongyu, Xu Kai, Liu Xueyi
Best for: xianxia fans who want devotion tested across time.
Not for: viewers who get frustrated by destiny plots and long separations.

Who Rules the World

2022 • 40 episodes
WuxiaPoliticsPower gamesPartners

Who Rules the World is one of the most accessible entry points into wuxia because it does not ask you to choose between a good romance and a good plot. Both are present, and neither one drags the other down. The two leads are equally capable and equally interesting to watch, which is rarer than it should be in this genre. You are not watching one person carry the relationship while the other waits to be rescued.

The early stretch has a lighter, more adventurous energy. The banter works because it comes from mutual respect rather than one person being helpless and charming about it. By the time the political stakes arrive and the power struggles step forward, the foundation between the leads is strong enough to survive the pressure the story puts on it.

The story leans more toward wuxia intrigue than heavy fantasy magic. Hidden identities, shifting alliances, and decisions with real consequences make the middle section genuinely engaging. The pacing stays controlled throughout without the long stretches of padding that can make longer dramas feel exhausting.

Watch it if you want a clean, entertaining wuxia with a partnership at the center rather than a rescue dynamic, and a story that keeps moving without asking too much patience in return.

Main cast: Yang Yang, Zhao Lusi
Best for: viewers who want a capable lead pair and steady pacing.
Not for: people who only want deep fantasy worldbuilding and realm hopping.

Love and Redemption

2020 • 59 episodes
XianxiaPast livesBig angstDevotion

This is the one to watch when you want xianxia that commits to the emotional heavy lane and does not apologise for it. Love and Redemption starts lighter than you would expect from something with this much angst in it. The early tone is warmer, which is deliberate. It needs you to care before it starts taking things away, and it is patient about building that attachment.

Once the story tightens, it tightens properly. Secrets, past lives, misunderstandings that cannot be resolved quickly, and a devotion that gets proved through loss rather than luck. The drama understands that the best xianxia romance is not about two people finding happiness together. It is about two people choosing each other despite every reason not to, repeatedly, under circumstances that keep making that choice harder.

The xianxia world is built solidly. Sects, spiritual cultivation, and big destiny beats are all present and serve the emotional story rather than distracting from it. But what keeps people watching is not the world mechanics. It is the relationship and whether it can survive what the story keeps throwing at it.

Pick this if you enjoy xianxia that earns its emotional payoff through genuine difficulty, where devotion means something because it costs something.

Main cast: Yuan Bingyan, Cheng Yi
Best for: viewers who want devotion tested hard before it is rewarded.
Not for: people who get worn out by repeated trials and slow resolutions.

Ashes of Love

2018 • 63 episodes
XianxiaImmortal realmsLove triangleSlow burn into pain

Ashes of Love is worth recommending specifically because of how it earns its emotional impact. The opening episodes are soft, almost fairytale-like. The female lead is sheltered and genuinely endearing, and the early romance has a warmth that makes the whole thing easy to fall into. That gentleness is not accidental. The drama is building something to break.

The shift happens gradually. The love triangle is not there for drama's sake. It creates real consequences because the people involved are all genuinely affected, and the show does not reset everyone's feelings after a difficult arc. The emotional residue stays on the characters and changes how they move through the rest of the story.

By the time you reach the heavier sections, you are already attached enough that the stakes feel personal. That is what makes this one of the more effective long xianxia romances. The sweetness at the beginning is the investment. The middle and the end are what the story does with that investment.

Watch it if you want a xianxia romance that begins like a dream and arrives somewhere much harder, with regret and real consequences alongside the love story.

Main cast: Yang Zi, Deng Lun, Luo Yunxi
Best for: people who want romance that starts soft and turns emotionally intense.
Not for: viewers who want a lighter, calmer xianxia from start to finish.

Battle Through the Heavens

2018 • 45 episodes
CultivationTraining arcRivalriesLevel up story

If romance is not what you are looking for and you want a fantasy story about earning your way back from the bottom, Battle Through the Heavens is the pick. The main character loses everything early on and has to rebuild. That premise drives the whole series, and it works because the progress feels real. Every milestone is visible. You can track how far the lead has come and feel the weight of each step up.

The cultivation world is structured around rivalry, training, and faction politics. There are always opponents to face, techniques to learn, and goals to reach. The pacing stays active because there is always a next challenge, which makes it easy to keep going without the story having to work too hard to justify another episode.

This is not a character drama in the traditional sense. The emotional depth comes from the climb itself rather than complex relationships. But within that lane it delivers consistently. Watching someone grind their way back to relevance in a world that wrote them off is satisfying in a way that more romance-focused dramas cannot replicate.

Watch it when you want cultivation fantasy with a clear underdog arc and the satisfaction of watching someone level up through effort rather than luck.

Main cast: Wu Lei (Leo Wu)
Best for: viewers who want training arcs, rivals, and clear progress.
Not for: people looking for romance or deep character study.

The Untamed

2019 • 50 episodes
CultivationMysteryLoyaltyMoral conflict

The Untamed built a massive following because it gets the central bond right. One lead is chaotic and fearless. The other is disciplined and measured. They balance each other in a way that feels natural rather than constructed, and that dynamic is what holds the entire story together even when the plot goes dark.

The mystery structure gives it something most cultivation dramas do not have. You are not just watching power levels and fight sequences. You are watching supernatural cases unfold, clan histories reveal themselves, and a conspiracy that keeps pointing back to something the characters were told to forget. The world has rules and the story respects them, which makes the stakes feel real rather than arbitrary.

As it progresses, the moral questions get harder. The drama asks what happens when doing the right thing makes you the enemy of everyone around you. That is where the emotional weight comes from, because the cost of integrity in this world is not small, and the show does not let the characters avoid paying it.

Watch it if you want cultivation fantasy with genuine mystery, a bond that earns every moment of loyalty it asks you to believe in, and a story that starts warm and becomes something much heavier without losing the heart that made you care at the start.

Main cast: Xiao Zhan, Wang Yibo
Best for: viewers who want mystery and loyalty alongside the fantasy world.
Not for: people who want immediate payoff and do not enjoy a slow build.
If you want a lighter, stylish entry point: Who Rules the World.
If you want maximum xianxia emotion: Love and Redemption or Ashes of Love.
If you want a cult favorite that turns into an obsession: The Untamed.
